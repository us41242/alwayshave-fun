## What was observed
- Official program pages are hostile to plain fetching: MGM and Station
  block simple fetchers, Caesars and Wynn hide numbers in JS-rendered
  sections. firecrawl got Boyd, Caesars earn-and-redeem, MGM earn/tiers,
  and the Station official rules; Wynn came through the plain fetcher.
  About an hour of the session went to sourcing, which is the right
  place to spend it on a page whose only value is being correct.
- MGM does not publish a gaming earn rate anywhere. Rather than borrow a
  third-party guess, the page says so and leaves the MGM cell blank in
  the calculator. That is a differentiator in itself: every competitor
  page I read either invents a number or skips the question.
- Station publishes tier names and the six-month cycle but not the
  thresholds; those came from Review-Journal and LVA forum reports and
  are footnoted as approximate. Boyd's Ruby tier earning zero spendable
  points is the single most useful fact on the page and no official
  summary leads with it.

## Learning
For sourced comparison pages, scrape the program's own rules page first;
marketing pages hide the numbers, rules pages spell them out (Station's
rules gave every earn rate and the 1,000-to-$1 redemption in one place).
When a program will not publish a number, publishing "not published" is
more valuable and more honest than any estimate.
