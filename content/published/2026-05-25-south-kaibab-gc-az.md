## What Late May Actually Means Here

The trail starts at 7,260 feet at the South Rim. It descends 4,780 feet over 7.1 miles one-way to the Colorado River at Bright Angel Campground — 14.2 miles total round trip if you're going all the way. That descent is continuous, exposed, and completely shadeless. There are no water sources on the trail. There is no creek to fill from, no pipe at Skeleton Point, nothing between the rim and the river except the rock you're walking on.

In October, the canyon temperature at mid-trail is around 55–65°F. In late May, the inner canyon runs 85–95°F by mid-afternoon, sometimes higher in direct sun on the canyon walls. The rim is 65°F today. By the time you're at Tonto Platform (4 miles in, halfway to the river by elevation), the temperature is 15–20°F higher than the rim. By the river, it's more.

The standard NPS guidance for South Kaibab in summer starts with: do not attempt a rim-to-river-and-back day hike in one day. That guidance applies to late May with about the same force it applies to July. The trail hasn't changed. The heat has arrived.

A 78 on the score is a "go, but be precise about what you're doing" number, not a "full rim-to-river no problem" number.

## The Water Situation

This cannot be overstated. South Kaibab has no water between the trailhead and the Colorado River. None. Other Grand Canyon trails (Bright Angel most notably) have water stations at Indian Garden and at the trailhead. South Kaibab does not.

The park calculates carry requirements at approximately one liter per hour of hiking in warm conditions. A 7-mile descent to the river in late May heat takes 3–5 hours depending on fitness and pace. The return climb takes longer. The math adds up fast.

If you're going to Skeleton Point (3 miles, 2,040 ft descent) and back — which is the recommended turn-around for a day trip — bring a minimum of 3 liters per person. In late May, 4 liters is better. Salty snacks to support electrolyte retention are not optional.

The mule strings on this trail have the right of way. Stand against the canyon wall and let them pass. This is not a suggestion, it's park protocol, and the mule handlers expect hikers to know it.

## What Trip to Actually Do in Late May

**For most hikers:** Ooh Aah Point turnaround (0.9 miles one-way, 600 ft descent). Gets you onto the trail, gets you the views, returns you to the rim before canyon heat becomes a factor. An hour and a half round trip. Good introduction to South Kaibab's geometry.

**For fit, experienced hikers:** Skeleton Point turnaround (3 miles one-way, 2,040 ft descent). This is the standard day-trip limit the park endorses for hot weather. Start at dawn. Carry 3–4 liters. Be back at the rim by 10am if possible. The views from Skeleton Point down into the inner gorge are the payoff — you can see the Colorado River from there on a clear day and you've earned the perspective.

**Rim-to-river in a day (14.2 miles total):** Not recommended in late May by the NPS, and I'm not going to recommend it here either. That's not a weather opinion — it's a heat physiology one. Come back in November.

## The AQI Note

AQI 64 is Moderate, driven by ozone. Same pattern as the Flagstaff and Sedona corridor in late spring — photochemical ozone builds through the afternoon on sunny days. Starting before 9am addresses this on the rim. Once you're in the canyon, you're in a deep rock corridor with decent air movement most days, and the inner canyon tends to be cleaner on ozone than the rim simply by virtue of elevation. That said, if you're hiking with anyone who has respiratory sensitivities, early start is the prescription regardless.

## No Dogs

Mark and Hank are not going. Dogs are not permitted on any trails within Grand Canyon National Park — this is park-wide, not trail-specific, not limited to paved sections. No exceptions, no permits that change this. They are staying home.

The South Rim village allows dogs on-leash on the rim-edge Greenway Trail, which runs along the developed corridor between Market Plaza and Bright Angel Lodge. That's the dog option here if your vehicle contains both humans who want to do South Kaibab and dogs who need a walk. The Greenway trail is paved, flat, and has canyon views. It is not South Kaibab. But it exists.

## John's Take

78/100 at South Kaibab in late May means the weather cooperated but the season is borderline. If you're here and you want to do this trail, do it: start at dawn, carry more water than seems necessary, turn around at Skeleton Point unless you're camping at the bottom with a reservation at Bright Angel Campground. The rim-to-river full loop is an October through April objective.

Tuesday through Thursday this week is the cleanest window in the 5-day forecast. Zero rain, AQI likely to improve as the afternoon ozone drivers ease off after a rainy Monday, and rim temperatures in the mid-60s. That's about as good as late May gets on this rim.

The canyon is not forgiving about poor preparation. Bring water. Start early. Turn around on schedule.
