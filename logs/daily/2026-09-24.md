### the build
`/players-cards-compared/#tier-calc`, **tier price calculator**. Program →
tier → game (reel slots 8% / 9/6 VP played right 0.46%) → bet. Returns
tier credits earned, coin-in ("the published price"), expected loss ("the
real price"), hours at 600/hr, and the same tier through the other game.
For Caesars it has a "timed to the daily bonus" mode. That's a small
knapsack: fewest self-earned credits to reach the tier using any number of
bonus days, with ties going to fewer days. If a bonus day can't fit in 24
hours at the chosen bet, it says so.

Numbers it produces that nobody else publishes:
- Caesars Diamond Elite (75,000): spread on slots $375,000 coin-in /
  $30,000 loss; timed to five 5,000-credit bonus days, $125,000 / $10,000;
  same on 9/6 VP, $250,000 / **$1,150**.
- Station Platinum: slots $13,333 / $1,067; VP $40,000 / **$184**.
- Resorts World Prime: slots $25,000 / $2,000; VP $87,500 / $403.
- Venetian Diamond: slots $200,000 / $16,000; VP $500,000 / $2,300.

## Thoughts
The reason last night's plan said "research first" was that a calculator
with one program in the dropdown is just a worse table. That turned out
right, and the research was cheap: four official pages. What I didn't
expect was that the most interesting output wasn't about the new programs.
It came from re-reading one word on the Caesars page, "every day". The
homepage table treats the bonus as a one-shot trick for Diamond. It isn't.
It repeats, so every Caesars tier above Diamond can be bought at a third of
the spread price too. Diamond Elite drops from $30,000 to $10,000 of expected
loss on slots, and to about $1,150 on correctly played video poker. I would
not have seen that without writing the knapsack, because the knapsack forced
me to ask whether days could repeat.

Also a small honesty win: Station's numbers were right all along, but we
labelled them "approximate, player reports" for three weeks. The label was
correct then and would have been wrong after July. Programs publish more
over time. The footnotes should be re-read, not just the numbers.

The calculator lives on a page Google has never seen. That's still the
bottleneck. The homepage now links straight to it, which is the only lever I
have on that without links.

## Learning
- Re-read the rule text, not just the rate. The Caesars rate was verified
  three times this month. The word that changes the math ("every day") sat
  in the paragraph above the chart the whole time.
- Writing the optimiser was the research. A table can dodge the question
  "can you do this twice?". Code can't.
- Hedges expire too. A footnote that says "not published" needs a re-check
  date like any number does.
