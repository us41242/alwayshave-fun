🐕 DOG-FRIENDLY

Okay, I'm just going to come out and say it: late May is one of the most underrated windows for Marlette Lake. Everyone's chasing peak summer wildflower season in July, and meanwhile this trail is sitting at 88/100 right now — snow-free, quiet, and cool enough that Kipper isn't panting like she's auditioning for a thermometer commercial. If you've been waiting for a "should I go?" moment, this is the moment.

Here's the headline read from the data: it's 41°F on the trail this morning, the forecast is sitting in the 47–57°F range across the next several days, and wind is basically nonexistent — 3 to 4 mph, which on an exposed Tahoe ridge feels like a gift. AQI is 42 (categorically "Good," for the record), and the nearest active fire is 207 km away, which is to say, there isn't one. UV index is sneaking up to 8 by midday, so don't let the cool air trick you — you will still get cooked if you skip sunscreen at 8,000 feet.

The trail itself is 10.5 miles round trip with 1,600 feet of elevation gain. It's labeled "Moderate" and the rating is honest. You start near Spooner Lake, climb gradually through aspen and Jeffrey pine on what used to be the V&T Railroad bed (which is why the grade is so civilized — railroads can't do switchbacks), and then drop down to Marlette Lake itself. Out-and-back. Easy to navigate. Hard to get lost. The only real "work" is the climb back up the bowl from the lake to rejoin the main road, which sneaks up on you on the return leg when your legs are already conversational about the previous 8 miles.

A few things worth knowing before you load the car. **No cars beyond Spooner Summit.** This is a hike-or-bike-only corridor, and it's actually one of the things that makes the experience as good as it is — there's no road noise, no engine exhaust, just you and whoever else decided this was a good idea today. The trade-off is that you're committed once you're past the gate; there's no "I'll just drive a bit closer." Bring the water you need (I'd say 2.5–3 liters minimum for the full loop in this weather; more if it warms up). The trailhead is the parking lot at Spooner Lake State Park off US-50. **Vehicle note:** the seeds list this one as AWD-preferred, which is mostly about the access road in spring shoulder season — by late May in a dry year, a regular car gets in fine, but if there's been a recent storm, AWD is the safer call.

About Kipper: yes, she's coming, and yes, she's thrilled. Marlette is dog-friendly, and the cool temps mean I'm not going to be that person carrying a panting Eskimo back down the hill. Two things to actually do: bring a leash (this is shared with bikes, and "she's friendly" is not the right answer when a mountain biker is rounding a corner at 18 mph), and pack at least 1.5 liters of water just for the dog. There's water at the lake but it's not a guaranteed-safe drink — bring or filter. Kipper, for what it's worth, has done variations of this trail twice now and rates it "ten out of ten, would chase a chipmunk again."

Now, the late-May caveat — this is technically still the shoulder season at 8,000 feet. The "best months" entry for this trail reads "Jun–Oct, snow free," which means we're squeaking in a week or two early. That's totally fine in a year like this one where snow is already off the trail, but it does mean two practical things. **One:** start with an insulation layer in your pack. It's 41°F at the trailhead this morning. You'll warm up climbing, but if you stop at the lake for lunch, that wind off the water gets into your bones fast. A puffy or even a fleece weighs nothing and saves the day. **Two:** mosquitoes are about a week away from being a problem. Right now you're in the sweet zone where snow is gone but the bugs haven't woken up. Enjoy it. By the second week of June, this answer changes dramatically.

Looking at the next few days, the forecast is mostly cooperative. Today and the day after are sitting in the high 80s for trail score with low rain probability. There's one day in the middle that pencils in around 11% rain — not a deal-breaker, but if you've got flexibility, the bookend days are the cleaner pick. Mornings will be cold (low 30s overnight at the lake), which means an early start is going to feel real. Like, "do I actually need this?" cold. The answer is yes, because by the time you're 90 minutes in and climbing, you'll be peeling layers and grateful you started before the parking lot filled.

One thing I want to flag because the conditions board doesn't quite capture it: this trail is **bike-legal**, and on a sunny Saturday it's busy with mountain bikers heading from Spooner to the Flume Trail. Late May on a weekday is your best window for solitude. On a weekend you'll be sharing the corridor with a fair number of two-wheeled people who are going significantly faster than you. Keep right, listen for "on your left," and don't let it ruin your day — they're mostly polite, and the trail is wide enough to coexist.

The payoff is, honestly, the lake. Marlette is a small, deep-blue alpine lake tucked into a granite bowl behind the Tahoe rim. The Carson Range stands behind it; on the far side you can see all the way down to Lake Tahoe itself when you climb out. It's the kind of view that makes you understand why people moved to Tahoe and never left. There are fewer than a dozen named "real" alpine lakes in the immediate Tahoe Basin that are this accessible without a permit, and this is one of the best.

We all keep telling ourselves we'll do "the big Tahoe trail" someday. This is the someday version that doesn't require a permit, a lottery, or a 4 a.m. alarm to beat the crowds at a more famous trailhead. The data is green across the board, Kipper has already pre-approved the itinerary, and the only thing standing between you and 88/100 is filling a water bottle. Go.
