THE PLAYBOOK

The core principle

You're not playing the house — you're playing the other five stacks. The house edge barely matters over 15 hands. All that matters is finishing hand 15 with more chips than everyone else at your table.

Survive cheap early, watch the stacks, make your move late with math — not vibes.

Hands 1–10: stay cheap, stay alive

Bet the minimum. Play flawless basic strategy — no hunches. Big early bets are the #1 amateur mistake: variance can bust you before the endgame, and early leaders get targeted by everyone's endgame math.

Count everyone's chips constantly. This is the actual skill of tournament blackjack. You cannot make correct endgame bets if you don't know the stacks.

Hands 11–13: positioning

Within striking distance of the leader? Keep betting small — your strike comes at 14–15. Far behind? Start swinging now, because one bet won't close the gap later.

Bet contrarian: leader bets big → you bet small (you gain when the dealer wins). Leader bets small → you bet big. You want your results decorrelated from whoever you're chasing. Copying the leader's bets locks in your deficit.

The chase formula (with numbers)

Leader has $2,600, you have $1,800. Gap = $800. They bet $500. For your bet B:

You win, they lose → need B > 300. Easy.
You win, they win → need 1,800 + B > 3,100 → B > 1,300. The hard case.

The bet that covers both winning scenarios:

Bet = gap + leader's bet + 1 chip = 800 + 500 + 25 = $1,325

Can't reach it? Doubles and splits are your extra gears — a bet doubled puts 2× on the felt. If it's still unreachable, cover the most likely winning path and know exactly which outcome you're rooting for.

Betting position is everything. Betting after the leader, you solve the equation. Betting first, you guess — assume they'll cover you, and bet gap + a big bet + 1 chip.

The flip play: betting to lose

You $1,800, leader $2,600 — but the leader bets $1,000. Look at the dealer-wins case:

Both lose → they fall to $1,600. Bet $175 or less and you finish ahead: 1,800 − 175 = $1,625.

So a minimum bet wins the table whenever the dealer beats everyone. In that spot, stand on your stiff 16 — you want the dealer to make a hand, not bust. You're not playing your cards anymore; you're playing the dealer's side.

The dealer wins outright roughly 48% of the time — better odds than winning a big bet AND needing the leader to lose theirs.

When you're the leader

The scenario that kills you: they win, you lose. Defense: match the biggest threat's bet. Both win or both lose → your lead is untouched; only the split result beats you.

Check their all-out maximum (stack + doubles + splits). If it mathematically can't pass you, bet the minimum and coast.

The 20-second final-hand checklist
  1. Gap = leader's stack − mine.
  2. Their bet — seen, or assume big if I bet first.
  3. Target = gap + their bet + 1 chip. Reachable? If not: bet + double, or split + doubles?
  4. If unreachable and they bet big: is min-bet and lose with the dealer the better line?
  5. If I'm the leader: match the threat's bet; can their theoretical max pass me?

Rehearse until this runs calmly in 20 seconds. That arithmetic under pressure is the whole tournament.

Quick don'ts
  1. No insurance (except a rare final-hand cover play).
  2. Don't bet big early to "build a cushion" — cushions attract five players' endgame math.
  3. Don't copy the leader's bets when behind.
  4. Don't hit into a bust when the dealer busting — or making a hand — already wins you the table.
  5. Don't lose count of the stacks. Ever.
