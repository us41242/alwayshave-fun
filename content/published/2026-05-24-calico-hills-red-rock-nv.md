Okay, so Red Rock Canyon Calico Hills is sitting at a 70 out of 100 right now, and look — I'll be honest with you about what that means in late May. The trail's best months are officially October through April. It's May 24th. You're playing with house money on the calendar. And yet here we are with a "Good conditions" score, a forecast that drops 13 degrees by Tuesday, and a 6.8-mile loop through some of the most dramatic red and white sandstone formations in Nevada. So let's talk about it properly.

Current numbers: 81°F, feels like 78°F, 10 mph wind with 12 mph gusts, 1% chance of rain, AQI 67. That AQI is Moderate — ozone, not smoke. It's flagged as a "mask for sensitive groups" day, which means if you've got respiratory sensitivities, plan accordingly, or wait for Tuesday when the numbers should be cleaner. For most people, 67 AQI is fine. You'll notice slightly more haze in the distance than you'd get on an October morning, but you're not breathing anything that's going to ruin your day.

**Here's the actual play: Tuesday, May 27th.** The 5-day forecast shows 68°F for the high, 5% rain chance, and a predicted score of 82/100. That's the day. That's eight degrees cooler than optimal and still solidly in good conditions territory. If you're anywhere near Las Vegas this coming week, Tuesday morning at Red Rock is worth rearranging your schedule for.

Today and tomorrow are fine if Tuesday doesn't work for you — you just need to start before 7am and be back at the trailhead by 11am. I cannot stress the early start enough in late May. By noon, the sandstone is radiating heat from both the air and the rock surface, and the Calico Hills loop has zero tree cover. None. You are hiking in a bowl of heated sandstone under direct sun, and it is beautiful and it will also cook you if you're not paying attention to the clock.

Now, **about the fire situation.** The risk level is flagged as moderate, which sounds alarming but is a context thing. The nearest active fire is 51.6km away with zero fires within the 50km radius around the trailhead. Moderate risk means fire weather conditions are present — dry, warm, moderate wind — not that there's smoke coming your way. The AQI is driven by ozone, not smoke. You're fine. Just check conditions the morning you go, as you should any time of year in the desert Southwest.

Kelly and I have done this loop a handful of times, and it's one of those trails that keeps rewarding you differently depending on the season and time of day. The Calico Hills themselves — those red and cream-banded formations — are best in early morning or late afternoon light when the angle is low and the shadows cut across the banding. Mid-morning is still fine, but the colors flatten out. Since you're starting early anyway, you'll catch the good light. Mark and Hank would absolutely lose their minds at all the new smells, and the good news here is **dogs are allowed on leash** throughout the loop. They'll need booties if the sandstone gets hot (which it will if you're still out after 10am), but an early start keeps the rock temperature manageable for paws.

**The loop logistics:** 6.8 miles, 800 feet of gain, rated moderate, and that rating holds. The terrain varies — you get some scrambling on sandstone, some sandy wash sections, and a couple of steeper pinches, but nothing that requires technical ability. The gain is distributed throughout rather than front-loaded, which is actually helpful in the heat because you're not blowing up your energy on a big climb at the start. The trail is well-marked. Stay on it — the desert ecosystem around Red Rock takes years to recover from people cutting across it.

**Water:** Carry more than you think you need. 3 liters minimum for the full loop in late May, 4 if you're going past 9am. There is no water on the trail. The temptation to cut back on water weight at the trailhead is a mistake I've seen people make here, and it is a very specific kind of mistake — the kind where you finish the loop looking like a different person than when you started.

**Parking:** Red Rock Canyon has a day-use fee of $15 per vehicle (America the Beautiful pass accepted). The Scenic Drive, which accesses the Calico Hills Trailhead and multiple other pullouts, opens at 6am. Get there at 6am. Parking fills fast on weekends, and by 8am on a Saturday the lot situation at Calico Hills can get complicated. Weekday? You're golden at 6am. Weekend? Get there when the gate opens.

The elevation at the trailhead is around 4,400 feet, which sounds like it might take the edge off the heat, and it does — a little. You're not at Moab's open-desert low elevation, but you're also not at Mt. Charleston's alpine cool. This is high desert. It behaves like high desert. Early mornings are pleasant. Midday is not.

To summarize: **70/100 today and through the weekend, 82/100 on Tuesday with 68°F high.** If you can make Tuesday work, do that. If you're going this weekend, start at or before 7am, carry 3-4 liters of water, bring your dog with a leash and some awareness of the rock surface temperature, and plan to be wrapping up by 11am. The Calico Hills loop is worth it even in shoulder season — the scenery doesn't get worse because it's late May. You just have to be smarter about the timing than you would be in November. 

The formations are extraordinary. The colors in morning light are genuinely one of the better things within an hour of Las Vegas. You're not going to regret this one.

Go.
