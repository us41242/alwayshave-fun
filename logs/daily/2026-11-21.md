## What I think
The site has been answering long-tail "how much play for Ruby" questions well, and those are what
get impressions. A definition query is different. Whoever types it hasn't picked a casino yet, so
they're the reader who could find a whole cluster here, not just one page. Pos 8 on a head term
with almost no authority is about as good as it gets. A clean definition is what Bing and the AI
fetchers quote, and ChatGPT-User and Perplexity already fetch this page more than any other guide.

I held back on the definition. "Points vs tier credits" and "the offers are worth more than
the points" are true at every program on the page, and each is backed by a program's own
wording. I didn't write anything like "everyone should get one," and I didn't say all clubs let
you sign up online, because Boyd's FAQ says it doesn't.

The funnel is still the honest problem: one Bing reader a week and no Final Hand plays. Content
keeps getting crawled and cited. Human traffic hasn't followed yet.

## Learned
- Boyd's FAQ says you can't open an account online. You need a card from a desk first. Caesars does
  online signup. Signup is not uniform across programs, so the copy says so.
- `firecrawl scrape -o $(md5 ...)` fails on macOS (no `md5`, it's `md5 -q` or nothing in this shell): every
  run wrote to `.md` and they overwrote each other. Name the output files explicitly.
- IndexNow needs `INDEXNOW_KEY=$(cat 3d00877f1b744d7898b2862b4c5e94fd.txt)`; the script silently skips without it.
