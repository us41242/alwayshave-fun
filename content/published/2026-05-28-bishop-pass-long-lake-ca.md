## What Bishop Pass to Long Lake Actually Is

This is the lower section of the Bishop Pass Trail in the Inyo National Forest, starting from the South Lake Trailhead in the mountains above Bishop, California. The Eastern Sierra version of "moderate" means 5.0 miles round trip with 1,000 feet of gain — sustained enough to feel like a workout, not so relentless that you're counting your steps.

The trail climbs steadily through lodgepole pine and granite, skirting the South Fork Bishop Creek before opening onto Long Lake at around 10,650 feet. The lake sits in a granite basin below the jagged towers of the Sierra crest, and in late May with the last of the snowpack still catching light on the peaks above, it looks like the kind of place people visit California specifically to find.

Long Lake is the smart turnaround. The full Bishop Pass climbs to 11,972 feet and requires snow travel until late June. The lake itself is accessible once the South Lake Road opens — which it did in mid-May — and the trail to it is snow-free or nearly so by late May. Going to the pass right now would be a different, more technical trip. Long Lake is the trip worth doing this week.

## The Snow Reality

The trail to Long Lake will have snow patches, especially on north-facing stretches in the upper section. In late May this is manageable without microspikes if the snow is consolidated — but bring them anyway. They weigh nothing and the difference between a confident step on packed snow and an undignified slide is exactly the kind of thing you notice at 10,500 feet.

Snow above 10,800 feet through June is the note in our trail data. Long Lake sits just below that threshold, which is why the turnaround at the lake is the right call. The views from Long Lake are the payoff — no need to push the pass when the snow conditions up there are legitimately committing.

## Logistics

**Trailhead:** South Lake Trailhead, Inyo National Forest. Take South Lake Road (Forest Road 8S07) southwest from Bishop — roughly 7 miles of paved mountain road. The road opened mid-May and is currently accessible.

**Parking:** The lot fills on weekends in season. Right now in late May it's better, but aim for 7–8am regardless. You want the morning light on the peaks anyway.

**Permits:** Day-use hiking to Long Lake is self-registration at the trailhead — free and quick. Overnight permits are separate and require a reservation through Recreation.gov. Day hike permit is a non-issue.

**Time:** Plan 2.5–3 hours up, 2 hours down, plus time at the lake. A full morning is the right block. Don't rush it.

**Gear for today's conditions:** If you're going out while the score is still borderline — microspikes, waterproof boots, insulated layers, rain shell. Not optional. The cold here is real cold, not coastal California 55-degree "cold."
