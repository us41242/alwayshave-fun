## What Eagle Lake Actually Is

Eagle Lake sits at 6,650 feet in the Desolation Wilderness just west of South Lake Tahoe. The trail gains 400 feet over 1.0 mile from the Eagle Falls Trailhead off Highway 89. It's 2.0 miles out and back.

That sounds like nothing. Here's the thing: the lower section of this trail is a genuine scramble. The first 0.3 miles up from the parking area is steep, rocky, and in places you're using your hands. It's not technical climbing but it's not a groomed path either. Riley handled it fine last fall. My neighbors' Labrador, not so much — he's a city dog and the rocks made him nervous. If your dog isn't comfortable on uneven terrain, that lower section will be a project.

After the scramble, the trail opens up and you're in the Wilderness. The lake appears at the end of a granite basin — water so clear you can see the bottom from the shore. The surrounding peaks still have some snow patches. In late May, the wildflower season is just starting at the lower elevations around the lake.

This hike is short enough that people discount it. Don't. At 62°F with AQI 11 and no wind, the views from the shoreline are the kind that make you sit down and stop scrolling for a while.

## The Permit Situation — Pay Attention to This

Desolation Wilderness requires a day-use permit from June 15 through September 30. The permit is self-issued at the trailhead or pre-booked through Recreation.gov. It costs $5 per person per day during that window.

**We are at May 24. The permit window does not open for 22 more days.**

This means zero permit friction right now. Show up, hike, leave. No advance reservation, no self-registration, no fee. This is rare for any Desolation Wilderness trailhead in warm weather and it will be gone by mid-June when the weekends start looking like a parking lot and the permit limit means some people get turned away.

If you're planning a trip to Tahoe or you're already in the area, this is the window to use.

## Logistics

**Trailhead:** Eagle Falls Trailhead, off Highway 89 on the west shore of Lake Tahoe between Emerald Bay and Meeks Bay. There's a small day-use parking area right off the highway.

**Parking:** Day-use fee applies (America the Beautiful pass accepted). The lot fills fast on summer weekends — in late May it's much better, but still don't roll in at 10am on a Saturday expecting an easy spot.

**Dogs:** Allowed on leash throughout the Eldorado NF and Desolation Wilderness section of this trail. Riley was in full lunatic mode from the first granite scramble to the lake and back. Bring water for your dog — the lake water is clean enough for them to drink at the shore.

**Cell service:** Unreliable at the trailhead and nonexistent once you're in the Wilderness. Download your maps beforehand.

**What to bring:** Layers. At 62°F with no wind it feels warm while you're moving, but you'll cool down fast when you stop at the lake. Wind shell in the pack. Traction not needed right now — the snowpack has melted enough at 6,650 feet that the trail is clear. There may be one or two small snow patches near the lake. Nothing that requires microspikes.

**Water:** The hike is only 2.0 miles, but bring 1.5–2 liters anyway. The scramble on the way up generates more effort than the distance implies.
