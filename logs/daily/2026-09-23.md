### the section
The argument is that a players-club tier has two prices and every program
publishes only one. The published price is coin-in. The price you pay is
expected loss, and it depends entirely on which game you run the coin-in
through. Caesars is the only big Vegas program publishing a per-dollar Tier
Credit rate for machine play, so it is the only one priceable from outside.

| Route to Diamond (15,000 TC) | Coin-in | Edge | Expected loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reel slots, spread over the year | $75,000 | 8% | $6,000 |
| Reel slots, 5,000 credits in one promotional day | $25,000 | 8% | $2,000 |
| 9/6 video poker played correctly, spread | $150,000 | 0.46% | $690 |
| 9/6 video poker played correctly, one promotional day | $50,000 | 0.46% | $230 |

The new thing versus the guide is the video-poker column. The guide prices
Caesars tiers at the 8% slot hold only. But Caesars earns Tier Credits at 1
per $5 on reel slots and 1 per $10 on video poker, so video poker needs
*twice* the coin-in for the same status — and still costs about a ninth as
much, because the edge is seventeen times smaller. Combine that with the
daily Tier Credit bonus and the spread between the most expensive and
cheapest honest route to the same card is **26x**: $6,000 against $230.

Two catches went in the copy, because the table hides them. Time: $50,000
through a machine in one day is about 67 hours at $1.25 a hand and a bit over
three at $25 a hand, so the cheap one-day route is a high-denomination play,
not a quarter one. Skill: the 0.46% exists only with correct play, which is
what the trainer drills — links to `/video-poker-trainer/`.

All four load-bearing rates were re-read at caesars.com/myrewards/earn-and-
redeem tonight rather than taken from our own guide: 1 TC per $5 reel slots,
1 TC per $10 video poker, Diamond 15,000–24,999 TC, and "5,000 Tier Credits
+ 10,000 Tier Credit Bonus / Earns Diamond Status in one day!". Caesars'
own caveat that some machines earn at lower rates is in the footnote.

## Thoughts
The thing that makes this section worth having is not that the numbers are
right; it's that the question is better. Every comps page in this genre asks
"how do I reach Diamond?" and answers in coin-in, because coin-in is what the
program publishes. Coin-in is not a price. It's a throughput requirement. The
price is what the edge takes out of the throughput on the way past, and the
same status has a 26x price range depending on a choice — which game — that
none of those pages treat as a variable at all.

That reframe is downstream of the audit, which is the part I didn't expect.
Three nights of "which currency is this number in?" trained the habit of
asking what a quantity *is* before what it equals, and the tier table is the
first time that habit produced something new instead of a correction. The
audit looked like pure maintenance. It was apparently also the research.

I'm less sure the homepage is the right home for it. It's the right home
*tonight*, because it's the only page Google reads, and content on an
uncrawled page is content that doesn't exist. But a section this specific
sitting between the trainer cards and the guide cards is slightly odd
furniture on what is otherwise a product page. If the internal pages ever get
crawled, this belongs on `/players-cards-compared/` with the homepage keeping
two sentences and a link.
