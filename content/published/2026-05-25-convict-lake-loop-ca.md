## Why This Trail

Convict Lake Loop doesn't ask much of you. It's 2.7 miles, 200 feet of gain, a loop around a lake at 7,583 feet elevation. Easy is the right rating. The effort required is low. What the effort gets you back for that investment is not proportional — the cirque walls above Convict Lake rise nearly 4,000 feet from the water surface, straight up, red and gray and white-streaked with snow that's still on the peaks in late May. The scale is Sierra Nevada scale and this trail doesn't make you earn it.

This is the kind of place where people do one loop, eat their lunch on the rocks at the far shore, and then do another loop because leaving feels wrong.

## Altitude Note

7,583 feet is not nothing. If you're driving up from LA or the Bay Area, that's a significant elevation jump in a short time. Convict Lake is an easy hike, but altitude affects people at easy hikes too — headaches, fatigue, breathing a bit harder on the climbs. Drink water on the drive up. Don't start the loop immediately after arriving if you feel off. Give yourself 30 minutes at the lake before you move.

Riley is fine at this altitude. He's done higher without complaints. Dogs generally handle elevation gain better than people do, just watch for unusual panting.

## Riley Situation

He's coming. Inyo National Forest is dog-friendly and leash requirements apply at the lake area. Riley gets the full loop, ideally with a stop at the shoreline rocks where the trail comes around the far end. He'll want to wade in. The water is snowmelt-cold in late May, probably 45–50°F, which he considers an invitation rather than a deterrent.

Pack extra water for him. The lake is accessible but I still carry a collapsible bowl and filtered water for the first hour of the trail when we're moving. Bring poop bags — pack everything out. The campground area is managed and the trail around the lake stays clean because people actually do pack out here.

## The Trailhead Situation

The access road to Convict Lake is paved, off Convict Lake Road from US-395, about 5 miles south of Mammoth Lakes. Easy to find. There's a resort and campground at the lake — parking is in the day-use area near the resort. No trailhead fee for the loop itself (you're in Inyo NF, not a fee area).

Mammoth Lakes is 15 minutes up the road if you need gas, food, or gear. June Lake is about 20 minutes north and worth the detour if you want to extend the weekend. The Eastern Sierra on a 100/100 weekend in late May is not something to rush.

## The Air Quality

AQI 19 deserves a sentence. That's not just "good" — that's exceptional. The Eastern Sierra runs clean air most of the year because it's high, dry, and upwind of the San Joaquin Valley. In late May before fire season opens and before smoke starts moving from anywhere, the air at Convict Lake is the cleanest air you can breathe in California. Bring your camera. Everything is in focus. The peaks are sharp. The reflections on the lake surface will look better than you expect.

Sunrise is at 5:38am and sunset is at 8:06pm. That's a long photography day. Morning light hits the western cirque wall first — the steep faces north of the lake catch the early sun and turn amber. Evening light swings back and hits the upper snowfields around 7:30–7:45pm. Both are worth it.
