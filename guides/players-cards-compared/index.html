Players cards compared: what each one really pays

Every Vegas casino wants you to carry its card. Only some of them tell you what it earns. Here are the five programs that cover most of the Strip and the locals market, with their published earn rates turned into the one number that matters: cents back per $100 you put through the machine.

How the rebate number is computed

Rebate = (points per $1 of coin-in) × (dollars per point)
Caesars slots: 1 Reward Credit per $5, 100 RC per $1
= 0.2 RC per $1 × $0.01 per RC = 0.2%, or 20 cents per $100 played.

Station video poker: 1 point per $2, 1,000 points per $1
= 0.5 points per $1 × $0.001 per point = 0.05%, or 5 cents per $100 played.

Two things fall out of that arithmetic. First, the whole spread across these five programs is 0.05% to 0.2%. Fifteen basis points is the entire difference between the "best" and "worst" base card. Second, that number is dwarfed by the house edge you are paying to earn it: a 0.46% video poker game costs you five to nine times what the card gives back on video poker (0.05% to 0.1%), and an 8% penny slot costs forty to eighty times the slot rebate (0.1% to 0.2%). The comps guide walks through why: the card is a rebate on your expected loss, and no rebate turns that loss positive.

The published rate is also the floor, not the ceiling. Tier multipliers (MGM's Slot Dollars bonus is 10% at Pearl, 20% Gold, 30% Platinum, 40% NOIR; Wynn adds 20% at Platinum and 30% at Black), point-multiplier days, and above all the offers in the mail add up to more than the base rate. The base rate is still the right way to compare, because it is the only part that is published, guaranteed, and the same for everybody.

Four Vegas programs publish a per-dollar Tier Credit rate for machine play — Caesars, Station, Venetian and Resorts World — so those four can be priced, and the tier price calculator below does it. MGM publishes a rate only for hotel-folio spend. Boyd and Wynn are left as their programs leave them.

Caesars Diamond is the tier most people chase because it waives resort fees and comes with a yearly celebration dinner. Spread across a year it is the tier most people over-pay for: 15,000 Tier Credits at 1 per $5 is $75,000 of reel-slot coin-in, and at the 8% slot hold the comps guide uses that is $6,000 of expected loss. Caesars' own daily Tier Credit bonus cuts the price to a third of that. Earn 5,000 Tier Credits in a single promotional day — $25,000 of coin-in — and the bonus adds 10,000, which is Diamond that day, for about $2,000 of expected loss. Same status, $4,000 cheaper, and the difference is nothing but scheduling. That is the comps guide's concentrate-your-play lever showing up as a published rule rather than a rule of thumb. It is still $2,000 for a status worth a few hundred in waived fees, so the advice does not change: earn it with play you were going to make anyway, or not at all.

Program by program

Caesars Rewards

Caesars Palace, Flamingo, Harrah's, Horseshoe, Paris, Planet Hollywood, The LINQ, The Cromwell, plus 50-odd properties nationally. Publishes the most: 1 Reward Credit and 1 Tier Credit per $5 on reel slots, per $10 on video poker, per $1 on hotel, dining, and retail. Live tables are rated. Reward Credits expire if the account earns nothing for six months. The national footprint is the real asset: a Diamond earned in Vegas works in Atlantic City and Reno.

MGM Rewards

Bellagio, ARIA, MGM Grand, Mandalay Bay, Park MGM, New York-New York, Luxor, Excalibur, and the regional MGM properties. Points are worth one cent and Tier Credits are 4 per $1 on non-gaming spend, but MGM does not publish a gaming earn rate at all: "varies based on your wager, game type, and game denomination." That is a deliberate choice, and it means you cannot compare MGM on the base rate. Judge it on the mail after a trip, not the point balance. What MGM does publish is the Daily Tier Status Accelerator, running through 2026-12-31 at the Las Vegas properties: a bonus on Tier Credits earned in one promotional day, up to 40,000 earned paying a 60,000 bonus. Like the Caesars bonus, it rewards concentrating a trip rather than spreading it.

Boyd Rewards

Orleans, Gold Coast, Suncoast, Sam's Town, Fremont, California, Main Street Station, Aliante, Cannery, and Boyd's regional casinos. One point per $1 on slots, per $2 on video poker, 1,000 points to the dollar. The catch nobody puts on the sign-up flyer: the entry Ruby tier earns no points. You need 750 Tier Credits before the card pays anything spendable, and Boyd does not publish how fast play earns Tier Credits — it varies by game, denomination, and time played, so there is no way to price that from outside.

Station Casinos Boarding Pass

Red Rock, Green Valley Ranch, Durango, Palace, Boulder, Sunset, Santa Fe, Texas, and the Wildfire locations. Same 1-per-$1 slots, 1-per-$2 video poker, 1,000-to-the-dollar structure as Boyd, with one difference that matters to locals: points can be taken as cash back, not only free play. Live table games have earned no points since April 2020. Tier status is judged every six months, so it is easier to hold than a calendar-year program if you play year-round and harder if you visit once.

Wynn Rewards

Wynn and Encore Las Vegas only. 1 point per $5 on slots; video poker is 1 per $10 or 1 per $20 depending on the machine, so the same $100 earns half as much on a full-pay game. 500 points buys $5 of FREE CREDIT, which is one cent a point, the same base slot rebate as Caesars, and Platinum adds 20% to points earned while Black adds 30%. Three tiers: Red 0–6,999, Platinum 7,000–69,999, Black 70,000+. Tier Credits are a different currency from those slot points and Wynn publishes no earn rate for them — "based on average bet, type of game you play, and length of play" — so the climb to Black cannot be priced from the outside.

The smaller clubs that pay more

The five big programs cluster at 0.05% to 0.2%. Downtown and locals independents publish rates several times higher, because they are competing for exactly the player who reads this page. Vegas Advantage's 2025 survey lists South Point at 0.3% on slots and video poker, Rio Rewards at 0.8% on slots, and Binion's and Four Queens (All Access) at 0.5% cash plus 1% in comps on slots. If your trip is about squeezing the card rather than the property, that is where the arithmetic points. If your trip is about the pool at Bellagio, no rebate changes that math.

Which card to carry

  1. Carry the card of wherever you actually play. Every program is free to join and the base rebate is small. The rate difference between cards is worth less than an hour of unrated play.
  2. Concentrate. Offers are driven by average daily theo on one account, as the comps guide explains. Two cards with half the play each get you two mediocre mailers.
  3. Take the new-member deal, once. Each program probes new accounts with its best offers. That is worth more than the points.
  4. Never chase a tier with money you would not otherwise bet. Read the tier table above as a price list and decide whether resort-fee waivers are worth thousands in expected loss. Usually they are not.
  5. Tournaments are the exception. Invitational blackjack tournaments come out of the same offer budget and pay real prize money for a fixed entry, or none. Learn the math and practice the final hand before you play one.
