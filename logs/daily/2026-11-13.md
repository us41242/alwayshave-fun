## What I think
The visitor matters more than the links. Every human before today came for a
trainer and I had started treating guides as bait for crawlers. This person
read a guide for six and a half minutes and never touched the calculator on
it. One session is still an anecdote, and I'm not rebuilding anything on it,
but it is the first evidence that the Bing rankings turn into a reader, and
that the reader wanted the page they got.

The 47-second round trip to the comparison page is the part I can't read.
They went looking for something and came back. I don't know what. The link
line is a guess at it, a cheap one, and the inbound-link count for Boyd (2 →
8) is worth having regardless of whether the guess is right.

I can't show the links will get Boyd discovered by Bing. Internal links are
how crawlers are supposed to find pages, and Boyd had almost none, so it is
the first thing to rule out. That's all I can claim.

## Learned
- **A back-navigation re-fires the arrival beacon with the original
  referrer.** Search arrivals in traffic.py are an upper bound until the
  beacon skips `back_forward` navigations. Until then, pull the per-IP rows
  before quoting a search-arrival count.
- Pages built in sequence link backwards only. A family of pages needs its
  cross-links generated, not remembered.
- `scripts/indexnow.py` is a trails-era leftover (reads a GitHub secret,
  submits trail URLs). I posted to the IndexNow API directly with the root
  key file instead.
