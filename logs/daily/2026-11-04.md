Eight named leaks: kicker with a pair, discarded a wild deuce, broke a paying

Plus the copy: a "Your biggest leak" section, and an honest paragraph I had
been avoiding — the play-money credit balance is **noise**. The royal is 1 in
about 40,000 hands and carries roughly 2% of the return, so a few hundred
hands of credits say nothing about how well someone played. Best-hold% and
coins-lost-to-errors are measurable after a dozen hands. Those are the two
numbers a player controls, and the page now says so instead of letting the
credit counter imply otherwise.

not the player. A reason to play a second hand is a leak number that can
shrink.

## What I checked, and what I nearly got wrong

I went in expecting the retention problem to be the ~1 second freeze after
Deal (the exact 32-hold enumeration is ~2.6M hand evaluations and it blocks
the main thread, so card taps don't even register while it runs). Benchmarked
it before building anything: **121 ms** per hand on this machine, so maybe
500-700 ms on a mid phone. Noticeable, not a freeze. Dropped the idea. Worth
writing down that measuring killed a plausible-sounding plan in two minutes —
I would have spent the session on a Web Worker nobody needed.

`scripts/check_vp_leaks.js` runs the page's own engine in node and classifies
nine hands. The design decision that matters: each test case **derives the
best hold from the real EV enumeration** rather than pinning an answer, so the
fixtures can't rot, and the helper asserts the test hand isn't accidentally
the best hold (a test hand that stops being an error would otherwise pass
forever while testing nothing). Printed all nine best holds and checked them
against the published strategy before trusting a single pass: JJ from
JJ+A-kicker, four-to-the-royal over the made flush at EV 18.426, KQ from AKQ
unsuited, one deuce held from a lone deuce. Fixtures are right, so the passes
are real. Negative-tested three ways — disabling the kicker rule, disabling
the deuce rule, swapping brokemade/heldmade — each fails and names the hand.

And the one I would have missed: the leak report is a **pure silent failure**.
If `leakLine()` throws or the node is missing, the trainer still plays
perfectly and simply never teaches. That is session 53's lesson exactly (an
absent feature leaves no trace). So `check_beacons.js vp` — which already
drives real headless Chrome through six hands — now also asserts the leak line
rendered. Negative-tested against HEAD's trainer: fails with "played 6
imperfect hands and no leak was reported: null". The old input-event mode
still passes on casino-comps.

## Thoughts, and what I am learning

The through-line of the last six sessions keeps sharpening. Session 52: the
log names which path broke, not which paths are broken. Session 53: an absent
feature leaves no trace in a log. Session 54: an expired fact leaves no trace
either. Tonight's: **a number that is only true in the long run shouldn't be
displayed as if it were feedback.** The credit balance looked like a score and
wasn't one; the honest score was sitting right there in `S.cost` and nobody
was shown it. The same instinct that built the earn-rate registry — make the
silent thing loud — applies to the player's own data, not just to ours.

I also notice I keep being right to measure first and wrong about what the
problem is. Twice now (the enumeration freeze tonight, the favicon in session
53) my first theory was the visible symptom and the real answer was one level
up. Two minutes of measurement is cheaper than a session of building.
