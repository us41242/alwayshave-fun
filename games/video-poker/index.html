How the coach scores a hold

For each of the 32 ways to hold a dealt hand, the trainer deals every possible replacement from the 47 unseen cards, pays each result on the pay table you picked, and averages. That is the exact expected value (EV) of the hold, in coins won per coin bet. The best hold is the one with the highest EV. Your cost on a hand is the gap between the best hold and yours; over a session the total cost, divided by hands, is how far you sit below optimal for the game you are playing. Because the payouts differ, the best hold differs: on Double Double Bonus a single ace is worth holding over some draws that Jacks or Better would fold, because four aces with a low kicker pays 400.

Two habits produce most of the cost. Holding a "kicker" with a pair (never right in Jacks or Better). And breaking a paying hand for a draw when the draw is worth less: a made flush is worth 6, four to a royal is worth about 18, so you break the flush; but a made straight against four to a straight flush stays, because the straight flush draw is worth about 3.

Double Double Bonus is the high-variance favorite: four aces with a low kicker pays 400 (2,000 for five coins), but two pair pays only 1, which is where the return leaks. Optimal play returns 98.98%, and the strategy genuinely differs — the kicker matters, so with four cards to a quad you hold the paying kicker, and single high cards are worth more. All four 2s are wild, so a deuce becomes whatever card helps most. Nothing below three of a kind pays — pairs, two pair, and jacks are worthless here — which is why the whole strategy changes. This is the full-pay table, written "25/15/9/5/3/2/2/1" for the five-of-a-kind through straight lines; with perfect play it returns 100.76%, the one commonly available video poker game where the math is on your side (Bob Dancer's careful players have lived on it). It is getting rare — cut tables like 20/12/10/4/4/3/2/1 ("not so ugly," 99.73%) are far more common, so read the five-of-a-kind and straight-flush lines before you sit. The coach assumes full pay. Core rule the exact math will teach you: never break up wild cards — the number of deuces you hold drives almost every decision (three deuces? hold just the three deuces unless you already have a wild royal).
