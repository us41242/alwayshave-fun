## What This Hike Actually Is

El Moro is the inland canyon section of Crystal Cove State Park. The 4.4-mile out-and-back I'm writing about today runs from the El Moro Visitor Center (across PCH from the main beach) up through the canyon and back. 400 feet of elevation gain. Easy rating, real. You can do this in trail runners and a small water bottle.

The version that gets the magazine-cover photos is the beach side — Reef Point down to Pelican Point on the bluff trail, with the option to drop down to the sand and walk it as a loop back along the tide line if you time the tides right. Both versions launch from the same general parking situation across PCH from each other, so the question is really: canyon, or coast? Or, on a 4.4-mile day with this kind of weather, both.

I'm going with the coast loop today because the light at 8 a.m. on a clear May morning on a bluff facing west-southwest is what I came here for. The canyon is better at 2 p.m. when the bluff trail is getting hammered by direct UV.

## The Real Story: Parking

I am going to be honest with you. The conditions are 100. The trail is a 100. The *parking* on Memorial Day weekend is a 35 with a steep downward forecast.

Crystal Cove State Park has three day-use lots: Reef Point, Pelican Point, and Los Trancos (the El Moro canyon side). They charge $15/day for parking, cash or card at the kiosk. On a normal Saturday in May, the lots fill by 9:30 a.m. On Memorial Day weekend with this kind of weather, the lots will be full by 8:00 a.m. and a State Parks employee will be turning cars around at the PCH entrance by 8:30.

Your options, in order of how badly you want to be on this trail:

1. **Arrive by 7:30 a.m.** This is the right answer. Sunrise is 5:46 a.m. this weekend; civil twilight gets you on PCH in usable light by 5:20. If you live within an hour of the park you can absolutely be in the Reef Point lot at 7:15 with coffee. This is also when the light is best for photos on the bluff.

2. **Park in Newport Coast and walk in.** There's a free residential parking situation off Newport Coast Drive on the east side of PCH that you can walk down to El Moro from in about 10 minutes. Read the signs carefully — some of the streets are 2-hour limit weekday-only, some are full no-parking. I've used Coast Royale Drive without issue but I'm not promising anything; check the curb signs.

3. **Park at Crystal Cove Promenade and walk.** The Promenade is the strip mall on the inland side of PCH at Crystal Cove. Their parking is for shoppers and they enforce it on weekends — but if you grab coffee at Javier's or breakfast at Pelican Hill before your hike, you're a customer. Walk takes about 8 minutes to the Reef Point trailhead via the underpass.

4. **Take the OCTA bus.** Route 1 runs PCH and stops at Crystal Cove. Doable from Newport Beach or Laguna with a $2 fare. I have never done this for a hike but it exists and on a weekend like this it might be the actual move.

What you should not do: drive in at 10:30 a.m. on Saturday and assume a spot will open up. People wait an hour for parking here on holiday weekends. You will be hot and frustrated and the conditions will already be past the morning sweet spot.

## The Trail Itself

From Reef Point, the bluff trail heads south along the cliff edge. It's wide, sandy in places, and obvious — you cannot get lost. About a half mile in, there's a staircase down to the beach at Pelican Point. This is where the loop choice happens.

If you stay on the bluff, you continue south to El Moro Beach proper, hit the turnaround at about 2.2 miles, and come back the same way. Total: 4.4 miles, 400 feet gain (gradual ups and downs along the bluff, never anything sustained).

If you take the staircase down, you walk the beach back north along the tide line — this is only safe at lower tide. Check the tide chart before you go. Memorial Day weekend tide is low around 6:30 a.m. and 6:30 p.m. with a higher high around noon. Morning hikers can do the beach loop coming back. Afternoon hikers should plan for the bluff both ways.

The beach section is the photogenic one. Tide pools at Pelican Point and Reef Point. Sea stars, anemones, the occasional octopus if you know what you're looking for. Tidepool etiquette: look, do not touch, do not move things. Crystal Cove is heavily monitored and the rangers will absolutely write you up for handling marine life.

## Why No Dogs and What to Do Instead

Crystal Cove State Park's dog policy is the strictest in the Orange County coastal park system. No dogs on any trail, no dogs on the main beach, no dogs in the historic district. Service animals only. Rangers enforce it. The reason is the tide pool ecosystem and the federally protected California least tern nesting area at the north end — dogs (and dog waste) are a real threat to both.

If you have a dog and you wanted this weekend to be a coastal Orange County hike with the dog, your options are:

- **Huntington Dog Beach.** Off-leash, on the sand, free parking gets tight but it's a beach not a trail. 30 minutes north of Crystal Cove.
- **Salt Creek Beach Park, Dana Point.** Leashed dogs allowed on the bluff trail above the beach (not on the sand). Coastal views, less crowded than Crystal Cove. 20 minutes south.
- **Aliso & Wood Canyons.** Not coastal but Orange County canyon hiking with dogs allowed on most trails. Laguna area. 15 minutes from Crystal Cove.

None of these are the same hike. But none of them require you to lie to a state park ranger.

## Bring

- 1L water for the round trip. Add another 500ml if you're hiking after 11 a.m.
- Hat, sunscreen, sunglasses. The bluff is fully exposed.
- A light layer for the morning marine air — gone by 10.
- Cash or a card for the $15 parking. Some kiosks have been finicky lately; cash is the backup.
- If you're going down to the beach: a towel and shoes you don't mind getting sandy.
- Camera or phone with the lens cleaned. The light here is what people drive an hour for.
- A tide chart screenshot. The beach loop only works at lower tide.

Skip the trekking poles. This is not that hike.
