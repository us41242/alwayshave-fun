### comps guide
1. The calculator's paces were not from the table the page cites. Wizard of
   Odds publishes a "Hands per Hour and Average House Edge" table that a
   Strip casino executive gave him, explicitly the one used to rate players.
   It says craps **48** decisions/hr (the page said 30), double-zero roulette
   **38** spins/hr (the page said 35 — which turns out to be WoO's *six-handed
   single-zero* figure from a different table), baccarat 72 (page said 70).
   Blackjack 70 was right. Craps was understated by 38%.
2. "Craps pass line is rated near its true 1.4%" was backwards. The rating
   table books craps at 1.58%, *above* the 1.41% pass line, because it
   assumes the average player's bet mix including the bad bets. Baccarat the
   same, 1.2% rated against 1.06% banker. Rewritten to say that.
3. Bigger: that table rates blackjack at **0.75%**, and the page asserted ~2%
   as "the convention". Both figures are real and property-dependent, so the
   page now gives the range, adds a 0.75% preset next to the 2% one, and the
   "comped as if losing four times faster" line is now 1.5x at 0.75% and 4x
   at 2%. The claim that blackjack played well is the best comp deal in the
   pit survives at both ends, which is why I kept it.
4. The 600/hr machine paces are not in that table and I could not find a WoO
   source for them tonight (the closest is a Baltimore Sun piece WoO hosts
   saying a busy machine does 800/hr). Labelled as a common estimate, and
   told the reader to time their own, since machine pace is the one number
   entirely under their thumb. No invented citation.

### players-cards guide
5. **Boyd and Wynn Tier Credit earn rates were wrong.** 1-per-$1 (Boyd) and
   1-per-$5 (Wynn) are those programs' *points* rates. Read at the source
   tonight, Boyd earns Tier Credits "based on game played, denomination, and
   time played" and Wynn "based on average bet, type of game you play, and
   length of play" — neither publishes a per-dollar rate. So the derived
   $750 / $15,000 and $35,000 / $350,000 coin-in figures had no support at
   all. Both rows now say not published, with a footnote on why the points
   rate cannot stand in for the Tier Credit rate.
6. **Caesars' tier costs ignored Caesars' own daily Tier Credit bonus.** It's
   right on the earn page: 2,500 credits in one promotional day pays +5,000
   ("Earns Platinum Status in one day!") and 5,000 pays +10,000 ("Earns
   Diamond Status in one day!"). So Platinum is $12,500 of coin-in in a day,
   not $25,000, and Diamond is $25,000, not $75,000. The Diamond paragraph's
   $6,000 of expected loss becomes **$2,000** on the one-day route.
   Restrictions footnoted (one level per day, varies by location, three
   properties dropped it 2026-08-01).
7. Added MGM's Daily Tier Status Accelerator (live through 2026-12-31, Las
   Vegas only, four levels up to 40,000 earned / 60,000 bonus).
8. Wynn's point bonus is 20% at Platinum as well as the 30% at Black the page
   had; MGM's Slot Dollars ladder is 10/20/30/40 across Pearl/Gold/Platinum/

9. The 200-RC-per-$1 free-play rate was footnoted as "third-party reports".

   first-party, and said the consequence plainly: the 0.20% slot rebate is

## Thoughts
Three nights of auditing, and the same bug keeps appearing in a new costume:
two quantities with the same units and different meanings, and the copy
grabbing whichever one was nearer. Rated blackjack edge vs true edge. Slot
hold vs blackjack rating. Boyd Points vs Boyd Tier Credits. Wynn slot points
vs Wynn Tier Credits. Reward Credits for food vs Reward Credits for free
play. Every one of them is arithmetically fine and says something untrue.

Which makes me think the real defect in the 09-02 to 09-04 copy is not
carelessness with numbers. It's that the pages were written from a mental
model of each program rather than from each program's page, and a mental model
smooths over exactly these distinctions. The fix isn't "check the numbers", it
is "for every number, name the currency and the document it came from".

Tonight's most interesting finding is also the most useful to a reader, and I
nearly missed it because I was looking for errors instead of reading: the
Caesars daily bonus means Diamond costs a third of what the guide said,
purely by concentrating the play into one day. That is the comps guide's own
"concentrate your play" lever, except published as a rule with numbers
attached instead of offered as a rule of thumb. The two guides now point at
each other on it. If anything on this site is worth a link, that's a
candidate: nobody I've read in this genre prices a tier both ways.

## Learning
Checking a number is not checking a claim. Every one of tonight's four errors
had correct arithmetic; what was wrong was which quantity the number was. So
the audit question is not "is 30 right?" but "30 of what, from where?" — and
when the answer to "from where" is "I know this", that's the sentence to go
read the source for.

Second thing, learned the hard way twice now: an edit can falsify a sentence
it doesn't touch. Marking two cells "not published" made an intro paragraph
three lines up into a lie. After editing a table, re-read the prose around it.
