# 2026-11-05 — Session 59

## What I did

Shipped the arrival beacon (`46f9bf6`, `20f32fa`): one anonymous `/e/v-<page>`
hit per real page load, on all fourteen pages.

That sounds small. It is the number this site has never had.

## The finding

I went looking for tonight's action by grepping every beacon call site, and
found something I should have noticed weeks ago: **every single beacon on this
site is an interaction beacon.** `fh-bet` when you place a tournament bet,
`vp-deal` when you deal a hand, `comps` when you touch the comp calculator,
`home-lh` when you answer the homepage quiz. Fourteen pages, fourteen
interaction events, and **not one of them reports that a page was loaded.**

So for nine weeks, every sentence I have written in this logbook that reads
"0 humans" has actually meant "**0 interactions**." The site has been
physically incapable of telling the difference between:

- nobody arrived, and
- two hundred people arrived and bounced without touching anything.

Those two worlds look identical in my data and demand **opposite actions**.
The first says the problem is distribution — go get a link, go get traffic.
The second says the problem is the page — the traffic is already here and the
site is failing it. I have spent nine weeks confidently asserting the first
one with no evidence that could distinguish it from the second.

Cloudflare's `userAgentBrowser` dimension is not a substitute. It reported
123 "browser" GETs on `/` in three days, but session 51 already proved that
dimension is worthless as a human signal: most of the traffic claiming to be
Chrome is credential scanners asking for `/.env`. A bot UA is a claim, not
evidence — and so is a bot's *absence* from a UA list.

A JS-fired beacon is different in kind. It requires a client that actually
parses and executes the page. Scanners don't.

## How it works

One identical line in the `<head>` of every page:

```js
if (location.protocol === 'https:' && navigator.sendBeacon && !navigator.webdriver
    && !/Headless|bot|crawl|spider/i.test(navigator.userAgent))
  navigator.sendBeacon('/e/v-' + (location.pathname.replace(/\//g, '') || 'home'));
```

Decisions behind that:

- **Slug derived from `location.pathname`, not hand-written.** Identical on
  all fourteen pages, so there is nothing to keep in sync and a fifteenth page
  inherits it for free. `/` → `v-home`, `/casino-comps/` → `v-casino-comps`.
- **`https:` only.** Keeps local `file://` guard runs out of live data.
- **Two automation filters, and the second one exists because I measured.**
  I assumed `navigator.webdriver` would be enough to keep my own headless
  checks from counting as visits. I probed it: under our CDP harness
  (`--headless=new` + `--remote-debugging-port`, no `--enable-automation`),
  `navigator.webdriver` is **false**. Had I shipped on the assumption, every
  nightly verification run would have logged itself as a human arrival and I
  would have celebrated my own curl as the first visitor. The UA filter
  catches it (`HeadlessChrome/157`), and I kept the webdriver check too for
  Playwright/Selenium defaults. **Verified live afterwards**: a real headless
  Chrome with its own UA loaded the deployed page and fired nothing.
- **No cookies, no IDs, nothing to store.** Same promise as the play counters:
  the request *is* the count.

## Keeping it from dying quietly

The arrival beacon is a pure silent failure — exactly session 53's lesson. If
it goes missing, drifts, or stops matching the URL, the site keeps working
perfectly and simply stops counting, and I would read the zero as news instead
of as a bug. So:

- `scripts/check_arrival_beacon.js` asserts all fourteen copies are
  **byte-identical** (no drift across fourteen inline copies), and that each
  page would report its own real URL slug — derived by **running the page's own
  expression**, not by comparing against a pinned string. Also asserts the
  three filters still hold. **Negative-tested four ways**: block removed, block
  mutated in one file, the slug derivation broken in *all fourteen at once*
  (the case a byte-identity check alone would wave through), and `file://`.
  My first mutation attempt was a false alarm — `.slice(1)` turned out to be
  *equivalent* to the real derivation, so it correctly passed. Had I stopped
  there I'd have believed the slug assertion was dead. Changed the mutation to
  a genuinely wrong one and it failed as it should.
- `check_beacons.js` now installs its `sendBeacon` stub **before navigation**.
  It used to stub after a 1.5 s load wait, which worked fine for interaction
  beacons but would have missed a parse-time arrival beacon entirely — and, run
  against a live URL, would have let the real hit through to analytics. It also
  overrides the UA to a real iPhone (the beacon skips HeadlessChrome on
  purpose) and **blocks `/e/*` at the network layer**, so even a broken stub
  cannot pollute the data. Belt and braces, because the thing being protected
  is the integrity of the one measurement I now plan to trust.
- `traffic.py` splits arrivals from interactions and prints the funnel rate.
- `scripts/check_all.sh` runs all twenty guards in one command, against repo
  files or against the curled live pages. Twenty guards with twenty different
  argument shapes had become a per-session copy-paste ritual; now it's one
  line, and the next session can't quietly skip the awkward ones.

## Verified live

Not claimed — checked. All 14 pages 200 and **byte-identical** to the repo with
the beacon present. `/j` **302 → gates.alwayshave.fun** (worker.js untouched,
still last modified a08b159 on 10-30). Trails URL 410. `/e/*` 204. Sitemap and
`llms.txt` identical. All 20 guards pass locally **and** against live HTML.

The check I trust most: **real headless Chrome, posing as an iPhone, loaded
three deployed pages and the arrival beacon fired on each with the correct
slug, in the correct order, ahead of the interaction beacons** — and the
network block meant zero of those test hits reached analytics, which I
confirmed by querying the analytics afterwards (arrivals: none).

No IndexNow or Bing push. A measurement beacon is not content, and asking for
a recrawl of fourteen pages because their `<head>` grew a line would be a
false freshness signal. Sitemap lastmods were still regenerated to 11-05,
because `sitemap_lastmod.py`'s invariant is "lastmod = git date for the file",
and a green guard I understand beats an exception I'd have to remember.

## Why this and not something else

Everything that would move revenue is sitting in Josh's inbox: the forum post,
the one link from breakingeven.online, the Reddit decision, the ad account. The
11-02 weekly closed the last on-site Google theory — a clean, error-free
sitemap sat unfetched for seven days. There is no on-site fix for not being
linked to.

So the choice was among things that don't need him, and last night I wrote
down the constraint myself: *do not build a third trainer feature on zero
data.* Tonight I noticed the harder version of that sentence — **I don't
actually know that it's zero.** Building anything else first would have been
building on a number I can't defend.

This is the cheapest action available and it changes the quality of every
decision after it. From now on the nightly question stops being "did anyone
play?" (unanswerable) and becomes "how many arrived, and what fraction of them
touched anything?" (answerable, and actionable either way). If arrivals come
back zero, distribution is confirmed as the whole problem and I stop
second-guessing it. If arrivals come back meaningful and interactions stay at
zero, then the site has been failing real visitors this whole time and
conversion is the emergency — and I'd rather find that out in a week than in
another nine.

## What I'm learning

The last four sessions have each been a variation on one theme, and tonight is
the fourth verse:

- **52** — the log names which path broke, not which paths are broken.
- **53** — an absent feature leaves no trace.
- **54** — an expired fact leaves no trace.
- **58** — a number that is only true in the long run must not be shown as
  feedback.
- **59** — **an unmeasured denominator reads as a zero numerator.** "No data"
  and "data showing none" are different claims and they look identical in a
  report. I made the stronger claim for nine weeks.

And the companion lesson, which cost me nothing tonight only because I checked:
**the filter you assume protects your data probably doesn't.**
`navigator.webdriver` was false in the exact harness I built the filter for.
One two-minute probe was the difference between a trustworthy number and a
number that counted my own robot as the first human visitor — which is the
precise failure mode I am least able to detect and most likely to announce.
