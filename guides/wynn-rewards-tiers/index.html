Wynn Rewards tiers: every benefit, and what the free credit really costs

Wynn Rewards, at Wynn and Encore Las Vegas, has four tiers, not three: Red at 0 Tier Credits, Platinum at 7,000, Black at 70,000, and an invite-only Chairman’s Club above them that appears in Wynn’s terms but not on its tier chart. Like Boyd, Wynn publishes no per-dollar rate for Tier Credits (“based on average bet, type of game you play, and length of play”), so no Wynn tier can be priced from the outside. What Wynn does publish is the slot-point rate behind its FREE CREDIT, and that one can be priced exactly: $5 of FREE CREDIT is $2,500 of slot coin-in at Red, about $193.50 of expected loss on Strip slots, or $23 on 9/6 Jacks or Better.

Tier Credits come from “select” reel slots, video poker and table games, but not live poker, race and sports bets, or electronic table games, live-dealer or not. Some slot and video poker machines earn nothing at all; Wynn says to ask at a Wynn Rewards desk which ones.

The tier year

Tier Credits reset to zero every January 1. What you earn January to December sets your tier for February 1 to January 31 of the next year, and a tier reached during the year is valid from the day you reach it until the January 31 that follows. So a Platinum card earned in 2026 runs through the end of January 2028: about 14 months if you reach it in December, 25 if you reach it in January. Annual benefits (spa, dinner, cruise, classes, golf) are once per February-to-January year, and are fixed by your tier when you redeem: a Platinum member who uses the $100 spa credit and later reaches Black does not get the $200 one as well. If you expect to move up, wait.

What FREE CREDIT really costs

This is the one Wynn number you can multiply. Slots earn 1 slot point per $5 of coin-in, video poker 1 per $10 or 1 per $20 depending on the game, and every 500 points is $5 of FREE CREDIT at Wynn and Encore Las Vegas. That is a 0.20% rebate on reels, the same as Caesars, and 0.10% or 0.05% on video poker. Wynn’s tier chart adds a bonus on slot points of 20% at Platinum and 30% at Black; read as 20% and 30% more points, the reel rebate becomes 0.24% and 0.26%.

7.74% is what slot machines on the Las Vegas Strip actually held over the twelve months to May 2026, per the Gaming Control Board. That figure includes video poker, so reel slots alone hold more. 0.46% is 9/6 Jacks or Better with perfect play, which the video poker trainer teaches; a 1-per-$20 machine doubles the video poker column to $46.00 at Red.

Same $5 of FREE CREDIT, same Red card. On the reels it costs about $193.50 of expected loss; on a 1-per-$10 full-pay video poker machine it costs about $23. That is 8.4 times the price for the slot player, even though video poker earns points half as fast. And Black’s 30% bonus only takes the slot price down to $148.85, still more than six times the video poker price at Red.

Two clocks run on this money. Slot points expire after 12 months without activity, and once you convert them, the FREE CREDIT must be used within 30 days. You can’t earn slot points while playing FREE CREDIT, and the minimum conversion is 500 points.

What the terms add to the chart. The birthday FREE CREDIT has no published amount: it is “based on member’s rated play”, loaded on the first of your birthday month and gone at its end. The birthday dinner credit ($150 Platinum, $300 Black) must be used in your birthday month at a Wynn-owned restaurant, not Cipriani, Urth Caffé, Delilah or La Cave, and you visit a Wynn Rewards desk first. The spa or salon credit is one visit, one venue, used in full. The complimentary hotel stay at Black is up to three nights in a Resort King, but it is “subject to availability and discretion of Personal Casino Host, based on game play”, so it is a host decision, not an entitlement. The MSC cruise takes a $500 deposit at booking; Platinum and Black get it back as casino credit on board, and fees and port expenses are included for them but not for Red.

Read as money: Platinum’s two dollar-denominated annual benefits are $250 ($100 spa, $150 dinner) and Black’s are $500, before the cruise, classes, golf and comp nights, which Wynn doesn’t price.

COMP DOLLARS: no published rate, but published limits

COMP DOLLARS are Wynn’s second currency, for dining, rooms, spa, golf and some retail. Like Tier Credits they are earned on “average bet, game type and length of play”, with no per-dollar rate. Live poker earns them by time played. They expire 12 months after earning, and they are spent before any discretionary comp. What the terms do publish is how many you can hold and spend at Wynn and Encore Las Vegas, by tier:

TierMost you can holdMost you can spend in a day

Few players will reach a cap. Its use is that the terms use it to rank Chairman’s Club above Black, which the chart never shows. If you are in a Wynn “gaming discount or allowance program” (a loss rebate), you earn no COMP DOLLARS and no annual benefits. Wynn makes you choose one or the other.

Ways in without play

  • FoundersCard Elite → Platinum. Wynn’s terms give FoundersCard Elite members Platinum status, but its annual benefits (spa, birthday dinner, classes) unlock only after two stays of two or more nights at Wynn or Encore Las Vegas in the program year. The cruise still needs 7,000 Tier Credits of rated play. FoundersCard is a paid membership; we haven’t priced it and don’t link it.
  • Tier match: only through Encore Boston Harbor, and only valid there. The terms say a tier matched through an Encore Boston Harbor promotion is not valid at Wynn Las Vegas. Wynn publishes no match into its Las Vegas tiers.
  • The other direction works. Caesars’ Diamond Tier Match, for new Caesars members through December 31, 2026, lists Wynn Rewards Platinum and Black. A Wynn card gets you Caesars Diamond. Wynn’s own program offers nothing like it. Every Vegas status match, compared.
  • Military. Wynn’s Hero program lists free self-parking, Buffet priority seating and invite-only promotions for service members, which are two of Platinum’s resort rows, without the tier.
