## What Kanarra Creek Is

Kanarra Creek is a slot canyon hike in Kanarraville, Utah — a small town just off I-15 about 30 miles north of St. George. The town owns the trailhead and manages the permit system. You pay $12 through their website, park in the small designated lot just off Main Street, and walk through the actual town to reach the canyon entrance. It is a little odd. The trail is 4 miles out and back, 700 feet of gain, rated Moderate.

The draw: Kanarra Creek runs directly through a narrow sandstone slot that the canyon walls compress to shoulder-width in places. You are not looking at the slot from the outside — you are walking through it, wading ankle-to-thigh-deep in the creek the entire way in. This is a wet hike, full stop. Bring footwear you can get wet. Trail runners you don't love, water shoes, or Chacos all work. Do not wear hiking boots you care about.

There is a ladder about 1.3 miles in where a pour-off requires a fixed wooden ladder to continue into the upper canyon. It is not technical climbing, but it is vertical, and it is the point where some people turn around. If you make it past the ladder, the slot narrows further and the light becomes genuinely unusual — shaft light reflecting off the water surface and painting the walls orange and gold in ways that photographs technically can't capture, though everyone tries.

## The Permit Situation — Do This First

Permits are required and managed by the Town of Kanarraville:

- Book at kanarraville.org/kanarra-falls
- $12 per person
- Daily visitor limits apply — Friday and Saturday permits for a forecast like this fill up
- The permit specifies your visit date; check in at the trailhead on arrival

If you are reading this on Thursday and thinking about Friday's 100/100 forecast, go book the permit before you finish reading this. Seriously. I will wait.

The permit system is lightweight and functional — it takes about two minutes to book. There is no lottery, no months-in-advance window, no Recreation.gov complexity. It is a small town running a trail permit and they have made it simple.

## What to Bring

- **Water shoes or sacrificial trail runners** — the creek is the trail for most of the canyon. This is not occasional stream crossings. You are in the water.
- **Trekking poles** — optional, but the rocky creek bottom is uneven and poles add stability in the wading sections, especially with a pack
- **Waterproof phone case or dry bag** — the canyon walls close to shoulder-width in places and water splashes. You will want your phone for photos and it needs to not be ruined
- **Extra layers** — the canyon is significantly cooler than the valley temperature once you are inside the slot. At 59°F today, the shaded sections feel cold in wet gear. A compressible midlayer in the pack costs nothing and matters
- **Minimum 1.5 liters of water** — the hike is only 4 miles but the effort level in wading is higher than walking. Stay ahead of hydration

Riley is staying home. No dogs on Kanarra Creek — this is a trail policy and it does not have exceptions. I have checked more than once. The creek-and-ladder combination genuinely is not a dog situation anyway, but Riley would disagree about that.

## The Slot Canyon Experience

I want to be specific about what you are actually getting here because the photos on social media are technically accurate and still somehow undersell it.

The Kanarraville canyon is not a grand geological spectacle in the way that Antelope Canyon or the Narrows is. It is intimate. The walls close in, the light comes in at angles, the creek echoes, and the combination of cold water, warm stone, and narrow passage creates something that feels genuinely removed from the world outside. The upper section past the ladder gets tighter and the light gets stranger. Most people stand in the upper narrowing and just stop for a few minutes, which is the right thing to do.

The ladder itself: it is a wooden fixed structure, approximately 20 feet tall, and it is secure. You are climbing it with wet gear and possibly a pack. Take your time on the rungs, grip with both hands, and do not rush the person above you. It is straightforward. It is also vertical with water below. That combination surprises some people.

Come back down before the light leaves the canyon. The late-afternoon light at Kanarra Creek, maybe 4:30–5pm in late May, hits the upper walls in a way the midday light does not.
